# 22A. HERO FILM — CINEMATIC SEVEN-STAGE LEAD EMERGENCE SEQUENCE

The landing page includes a cinematic motion hero representing the **Lead Emergence philosophy and seven-stage progression**.

Treat the supplied hero-video description/storyboard as a **creative source of truth**.

Do not replace it with:

* generic floating UI;
* random AI particles;
* abstract gradient blobs;
* generic SaaS product-demo animation;
* a montage of dashboards;
* stock “business transformation” footage;
* unrelated career-transition imagery.

The hero is the emotional introduction to the broader Lead Emergence platform.

It should communicate:

> **Before you decide what to do, you have to see what is actually here.**

and visually move through the Lead Emergence progression:

**SEE → REFRAME → ALIGN → BUILD → PRODUCE → LIVE / NEW REALITY → SEE AGAIN**

The canonical stage framing is:

1. **SEE REALITY**
2. **REFRAME REALITY**
3. **ALIGN WITH REALITY**
4. **BUILD CAPABILITY**
5. **PRODUCE VALUE**
6. **NEW REALITY**
7. **SEE AGAIN**

The hero should feel like a **premium cinematic brand film**, not a software feature reel.

## Visual character

Preserve the current landing-page language:

* near-black cinematic environment;
* deep navy/indigo atmosphere;
* subtle warm light;
* restrained grain;
* shallow depth;
* deliberate camera movement;
* luminous cyan visual thread;
* restrained gold emerging later in the journey;
* typography integrated into the film rather than appearing like slideshow captions;
* elegant transitions with meaningful visual continuity.

Motion should feel:

* deliberate;
* spacious;
* controlled;
* slightly dramatic;
* intelligent;
* human.

Avoid frantic kinetic typography or constant movement.

---

## Narrative purpose

The film should show that transformation is not linear task completion.

The leader moves through a repeated process of:

**seeing what exists → questioning interpretation → finding alignment → creating capability → producing something real → inhabiting the changed reality → seeing again from a new position.**

The seventh stage should make clear that the process is recursive.

The destination creates a new vantage point.

It is not:

> problem → solution → finished.

It is closer to:

> reality → understanding → movement → new reality → deeper perception.

That recurring loop should connect directly to how the product operates underneath the brand.

---

## Visual continuity with the page

The hero should not end as an isolated video followed by a webpage.

Design the hero so its visual system can **escape the film and continue through the scroll experience**.

In particular:

### Journey line

The cyan progress line introduced in the hero should become the continuous visual thread of the page.

At the end of the hero it should be positioned so the page can visually continue it into:

* **Everything starts with the leader**
* contextual inputs
* the conversation
* Workspace
* ChatGPT
* example questions
* the final landscape/CTA

As the page approaches the final invitation, that cyan line may transition toward gold.

The handoff should feel like:

> **the film became the interface**

rather than:

> video ended → next website section began.

---

## Opening behavior

The hero may remain pinned for part of the initial scroll.

Do not require the user to watch a fixed-duration commercial before accessing the page.

The visitor should be able to begin scrolling immediately.

As they scroll:

* the film progresses;
* stage copy may reveal;
* environmental composition evolves;
* the page begins emerging before the cinematic environment completely disappears.

The next statement:

> **Everything starts with the leader.**

should emerge **from the hero environment**, not appear after a hard boundary.

---

## Stage choreography

Use the supplied detailed hero description/storyboard where available.

Where implementation interpretation is required, preserve this conceptual progression:

### 1 — SEE REALITY

Begin with ambiguity rather than explanation.

Show a complex environment in which information, signals, people, work, relationships, and constraints exist but do not yet form an obvious picture.

The viewer should feel:

> there is more here than I can immediately make sense of.

The system does **not** instantly solve it.

It observes.

The first movement is attention.

---

### 2 — REFRAME REALITY

Relationships between previously disconnected signals begin to appear.

Perspective changes.

The camera, composition, typography, or luminous path should reveal that the original interpretation was incomplete.

This stage should visually communicate:

> **The thing you first noticed may not be the actual problem.**

Do not use an obvious “AI analyzes data” cliché.

The transformation should feel perceptual.

---

### 3 — ALIGN WITH REALITY

Separate elements begin orienting around something shared.

Not uniformity.

Alignment.

Lines, spatial relationships, human signals, decisions, or information begin cohering.

The visual metaphor should communicate:

> clarity allows different things to move together without making them identical.

---

### 4 — BUILD CAPABILITY

The scene should gain structure.

Something previously conceptual becomes usable.

This may involve:

* emerging pathways;
* connections;
* architecture;
* conversational interfaces;
* workflows;
* coordinated human/system elements.

Avoid literal construction graphics.

The core idea is:

> **structure creates the ability to act.**

---

### 5 — PRODUCE VALUE

Capability begins producing visible consequence.

The system is no longer merely organized.

Something changes in reality.

That value should remain human-centered rather than becoming a graph shooting upward.

Examples of visual consequence may suggest:

* clearer decisions;
* completed movement;
* connection;
* opportunity;
* space;
* coordinated action;
* a person moving forward.

The product exists to change what people can do—not to generate more software activity.

---

### 6 — NEW REALITY

Allow the film to breathe.

This stage should feel different from the opening.

Not perfect.

Changed.

The leader now occupies a reality that did not exist at the beginning because prior movement created it.

This is where restrained gold can become more prominent.

The visual environment should communicate arrival without finality.

---

### 7 — SEE AGAIN

The camera or visual perspective reveals that the new reality contains new signals and new questions.

The journey line should imply continuation.

The final visual message is:

> **The better you become at seeing, the more clearly you can lead what comes next.**

This stage should naturally hand the visitor into the rest of the landing page.

# Relationship to SOTF Bundle

Do **not** turn the hero film into a military-transition commercial.

The hero represents the broader Lead Emergence philosophy.

The SOTF Bundle should demonstrate later on the page that this philosophy becomes operational in a concrete transition workflow.

For example:

**SEE**
A role looks attractive.

**REFRAME**
The real question is not merely whether the fellow qualifies, but whether the actual work fits.

**ALIGN**
Compare the opportunity against confirmed criteria, values, experience, and constraints.

**BUILD**
Identify the evidence, networking conversation, or preparation required.

**PRODUCE**
Take the next useful action.

**NEW REALITY**
The fellow now knows more about the opportunity and themselves.

**SEE AGAIN**
That learning changes the next opportunity recommendation.

This relationship should be discoverable through the product experience, but it does not need to be narrated literally inside the hero.

# Hero copy restraint

Do not overload the hero with explanation.

The film should create curiosity and emotional orientation.

Use short stage language, selective phrases, and strong typography.

The detailed explanation belongs further down the page.

Avoid:

* paragraphs over video;
* seven simultaneous labels;
* explanatory diagrams;
* feature names;
* integration names;
* SOTF feature lists.

The film should make the viewer **feel the framework before the rest of the page explains it.**

# Hero/page technical relationship

The final rendered hero should support:

* optimized web video delivery;
* poster frame;
* responsive cropping;
* pause behavior;
* reduced-motion alternative;
* graceful fallback if autoplay is blocked;
* sufficient contrast for text;
* no essential information encoded solely through animation.

Do not bind accessibility to video playback.

For reduced-motion users:

* use a strong hero poster/static composition;
* present the seven-stage framing through normal page content;
* retain the narrative order;
* eliminate scroll-scrubbing movement.

# Performance constraint

Do not sacrifice the landing experience for cinematic ambition.

Measure:

* initial payload;
* video format/size;
* LCP implications;
* mobile performance;
* CPU/GPU usage during scroll;
* loading behavior.

Use the film as atmosphere and narrative—not as an excuse for a 100 MB landing page.

Do not call the hero complete merely because all seven stage labels appear.
